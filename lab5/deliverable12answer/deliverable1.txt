LAB5 Deliverable 1 - Practice with Perspective Projection
=========================================================

Camera model: f = 1, principal point (0,0), s_x = s_y = 1, zero skew, so a point
(X,Y,Z) projects to (u,v) = (X/Z, Y/Z). A pixel (u,v) back-projects to the ray
lambda * m, with m = (u, v, 1), lambda > 0.

A pixel belongs to the projection of the sphere exactly when its ray hits the
sphere. The boundary of the projection is made of the rays tangent to the sphere.


1. Sphere centred at (0, 0, d)
------------------------------

Expected shape: by symmetry around the optical axis, a circle centred at (0,0).

Put the ray into the sphere equation |lambda*m - c|^2 = r^2, c = (0,0,d):

    lambda^2 (u^2 + v^2 + 1) - 2 lambda d + d^2 - r^2 = 0

The ray is tangent when this quadratic in lambda has a double root:

    d^2 = (u^2 + v^2 + 1)(d^2 - r^2)

which gives

    u^2 + v^2 = r^2 / (d^2 - r^2)

The projection is a circle centred at the principal point with radius

    rho = r / sqrt(d^2 - r^2)

and its interior (a filled disk) for the rays that cross the sphere. d > r + 1
gives d^2 > r^2, so the radius is real and the whole sphere is in front of the
camera. Note rho > r/d: the apparent radius is larger than the naive r/d, because
the tangent points are closer to the camera than the sphere centre.


2. Sphere centred at an arbitrary c = (a, b, C)
-----------------------------------------------

The same double-root condition, for any c outside the sphere, gives

    (c . m)^2 = (m . m)(|c|^2 - r^2)

that is, with k = |c|^2 - r^2 > 0,

    (a u + b v + C)^2 = k (u^2 + v^2 + 1)

This is quadratic in (u,v), so the projection is a conic: the cone of tangent rays
cut by the image plane. Its type comes from the determinant of the (u,v) quadratic
part, which equals k (C^2 - r^2):

    C^2 > r^2 (sphere entirely in front of the plane Z = 0)  ->  ellipse
    C^2 = r^2 (sphere tangent to the plane Z = 0)            ->  parabola
    C^2 < r^2 (sphere crossing the plane Z = 0)              ->  hyperbola

For the usual ellipse case, let rho0 = sqrt(a^2 + b^2) and use the axes x along
the radial direction (a,b)/rho0 and y perpendicular to it. Completing the square:

    (C^2 - r^2)(x - x0)^2 + k y^2 = r^2 k / (C^2 - r^2)

    centre           x0  = rho0 C / (C^2 - r^2), y0 = 0
    radial semi-axis A_r = r sqrt(k) / (C^2 - r^2)
    other semi-axis  A_t = r / sqrt(C^2 - r^2)
    ratio            A_r / A_t = sqrt(k / (C^2 - r^2)) >= 1

So a sphere off the optical axis projects to an ellipse whose major axis points
away from the principal point. It gets more elongated as the sphere moves away from
the axis, and for a = b = 0 it reduces to the circle of part 1 (k = C^2 - r^2,
A_r = A_t = rho). The ellipse centre is also not the projection (a/C, b/C) of the
sphere centre: x0 = (rho0/C) * C^2/(C^2 - r^2) lies further out.

Numeric check: with sampled tangent rays, for (r,d) = (1,3), (0.5,2), (2,3.5) the
measured radius equals the formula, and for c = (1,0.5,4), r = 1 and c = (2,-1,3),
r = 1.5 the tangent rays satisfy the conic (residual 1e-14), with the centre above
and the major axis along (a,b).
