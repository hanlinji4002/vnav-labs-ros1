LAB5 Deliverable 2 - Vanishing Points
=====================================

Camera model: f = 1, principal point (0,0), s_x = s_y = 1, zero skew, so
(X,Y,Z) projects to (x,y) = (X/Z, Y/Z), the homogeneous pixel (X, Y, Z).

A 3D line is p(lambda) = p0 + lambda u, with p0 = (p0x, p0y, p0z) and the unit
direction u = (ux, uy, uz). Parallel lines share u and differ in p0.


1. Vanishing point
------------------

The projection of a point on the line is

    x(lambda) = (p0x + lambda ux) / (p0z + lambda uz)
    y(lambda) = (p0y + lambda uy) / (p0z + lambda uz)

If uz != 0, let lambda go to infinity:

    v = (x, y) = (ux / uz, uy / uz)

The limit depends only on the direction u and not on p0, so all parallel lines with
direction u go through this same pixel: v is their vanishing point. In homogeneous
coordinates v = (ux, uy, uz), that is, v is the image of the ray through the optical
centre parallel to the lines.

Geometric check: a 3D line and the optical centre span a plane with normal
n = p0 x u, and the image of the line is this plane cut with the image plane, so
n is also the homogeneous image line. Two parallel lines give n1 = p01 x u and
n2 = p02 x u, and they intersect at

    n1 x n2 = (p01 x u) x (p02 x u) = [(p01 x u) . p02] u

because (p01 x u) . u = 0. Thus the intersection is u again, as a homogeneous point.
The scalar is zero only if the two lines are coplanar with the optical centre, and
then they have the same image line.


2. When parallel lines stay parallel in the image
-------------------------------------------------

Claim: two parallel 3D lines (not coplanar with the optical centre) have parallel
images if and only if uz = 0, i.e. the lines are parallel to the image plane.

If uz = 0: every point of the line has depth Z = p0z, so

    (x, y) = (p0x, p0y) / p0z + lambda (ux, uy) / p0z

which is a straight image line of direction (ux, uy), independent of p0. Lines with
the same u therefore have the same image direction: they are parallel. In homogeneous
terms they meet at (ux, uy, 0), a point at infinity.

If uz != 0: by part 1 both image lines pass through the finite pixel (ux/uz, uy/uz).
Two different lines through a common finite point intersect, so they are not parallel.

Numeric check: for u along (0.3,-0.2,0.9) and two different p0, the projections at
lambda = 1e8 converge to (ux/uz, uy/uz) = (0.3333,-0.2222), n1 x n2 is a multiple of
u; for u = (0.6,0.8,0) the image line normals are equal and n1 x n2 has zero z.
