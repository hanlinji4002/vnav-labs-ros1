Deliverable 6.a - Pair of frames
--------------------------------
  Statistics                SIFT    SURF    ORB     FAST+BRIEF

  # of Keypoints in Img 2   969     1822    4362    3653

  Inlier Ratio              80.4%   63.9%   66.7%   41.5%

What was tried and what worked best:
- ORB levels: 1 pyramid level (skeleton) gave 7 inliers, 8 levels gave 28, both with 500
  features. The box changes scale between the images, so the pyramid is needed.
- ORB features: 500 / 2000 / 5000 gave 28 / 56 / 80 inliers, at ratios 93% / 76% / 67%.
  5000 was kept, since the handout asks for the largest number of inliers.
- Matcher for binary descriptors: FLANN with an LSH index found the same number of good
  matches and inliers as the brute-force Hamming matcher, but took longer (ORB 500: 11.5
  vs 8.1 ms, FAST: 56.5 vs 15.0 ms). Brute force was kept.
- FAST threshold 5 / 10 / 30 gave 13 / 17 / 7 inliers. 10 was kept.
- SURF Hessian threshold 50 / 100 / 400 gave 89 / 85 / 74 inliers, at ratios 60% / 64% /
  64%. The default 100 was kept: 4 more inliers at a lower ratio is not a real gain.
- SIFT contrast threshold 0.02 added keypoints but no inliers (82 for both).

SIFT, SURF and ORB handle this pair. FAST+BRIEF does not: BRIEF is neither scale nor
rotation invariant, and box_FAST_inliers_outliers.png shows that even many of its 17
"inliers" are wrong. RANSAC on the fundamental matrix only requires a point to be within
3 px of an epipolar line, so wrong matches can pass by chance.

Times are from the VM and only useful as relative values: they also depend on the playback
speed (ORB took 7.8 ms per pair at full speed and 13.0 ms at half speed on the 30fps bag).
For descriptor methods a pair means detecting and describing both frames.

Conclusions:
- Between consecutive video frames the motion is small, so all methods reach high inlier
  ratios (95-99% and 85-95%), and FAST+BRIEF, poor on the box pair, works well here.
- Number of features: this depends on the scene. On the real 30fps bag ORB and FAST lead
  (952, 827 keypoints). On the low-texture simulated bag SURF finds the most (1193) and
  SIFT the fewest (148).
- Speed: SIFT and SURF are the slowest, about 2-4 times slower than ORB and 3-12 times
  slower than FAST+BRIEF in these runs.
- Use: SIFT and SURF suit large viewpoint and scale changes, such as the box pair or
  recognising a place again, when time is not critical. ORB is a good compromise for
  real-time use, since it stays scale and rotation invariant. FAST+BRIEF suits fast
  frame-to-frame tracking with little rotation in the image plane.


Deliverable 7 - Lucas-Kanade tracker
------------------------------------
Implementation: Harris corners (goodFeaturesToTrack, up to 1000, at least 10 px apart),
tracked with calcOpticalFlowPyrLK. Outlier tracks are dropped and new corners added.
Tracks at frame 100: 30fps_LK_tracks.png, smooth_LK_tracks.png. Entry: Harris+LK column above.

Assumptions of the LK tracker:
- Brightness constancy: a feature keeps its intensity between frames.
- Small motion: a feature moves little between frames (the pyramid extends this range).
- Spatial coherence: all pixels in the window around a feature move together.

Comparison with the descriptor methods:
- LK has the highest inlier ratio on both bags (99.3%, 94.7%) and is the fastest on the 30fps
  bag, because it searches near the old position and computes no descriptors. Its ratio is
  also helped by the implementation: tracks found to be outliers do not continue.
- It keeps fewer points than ORB and FAST+BRIEF. On the simulated bag only 156 corners are
  available, about as few as SIFT.
- It has no good-matches column, since there is no descriptor distance to apply a ratio to.
- It only works while its assumptions hold. It cannot match a wide-baseline pair like the box
  images, or find a feature again after losing it, which descriptor methods can do.


Deliverable 8 (optional) - Optical flow
---------------------------------------
Farneback dense flow in src/self_flow.cpp, run with optical_flow.launch on both bags.
Frame 100 next to its flow: farneback_30fps.png, farneback_smooth.png. Hue is the direction of
motion, brightness the magnitude (full brightness at 10 px). The flow is reliable on textured
regions and edges, while uniform walls show little or unreliable flow, which is the aperture
problem.
